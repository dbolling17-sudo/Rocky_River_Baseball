## The 2024 conference champions

The 2024 Pirates went 23-6, set a school record for wins, and won the program's first Great Lakes Conference championship with a 10-2 league record. After the fourth week of the season they reached No. 1 in the Division II state poll and stayed there for the rest of the year.

The lineup was balanced and athletic. Josh Mommers set the table at the top by getting on base and stealing bases. Anthony Ferchill and Thomas Rossi drove in runs in the middle of the order, Griffin Hill and Michael Perry added power, and Jeffrey Maslar contributed at the plate and behind it.

The pitching staff was one of the best Rocky River has had. On April 10, 2024, Rees Heigle threw the first no-hitter in program history, striking out nine over seven innings in a 5-0 conference win over North Olmsted. Left-hander Joseph Brant and co-captain Michael Perry rounded out a top three that head coach Ed Piazza said he would "put up with anybody."

The team got off to the best start in school history, including a 13-inning comeback win over Lakewood tied up by a three-run, inside-the-park home run from Rossi. In the postseason, the Pirates beat Akron Hoban in the regional tournament and reached the district championship game, where they fell short.

Seven members of the 2024 team went on to commit to college programs (see [Playing at the next level](#playing-at-the-next-level)).

## The 1998 state runner-up team

The 1998 varsity, coached by Jim Rucki, put together one of the best seasons in school history. The Pirates went 20-10 and played for the Division II state championship.

In the state semifinal, Rocky River beat Columbus Bexley 3-1. Jeff Werbeach and Mike Dickey each had more than one hit, and Chris Meyer pitched a complete game, giving up five hits and one earned run over seven innings. He outdueled Bexley's Doug Deeds, who was later drafted by the Minnesota Twins in 2002.

The Pirates fell to Cincinnati McNicholas in the state final and finished as Division II State Runner-Up. Rocky River had 10 hits in that game, with Drew Fortenbaugh and Mike Dickey each collecting more than one hit, including a triple apiece. Matt Brumbort pitched most of the game before Meyer came on to get the final batter in the bottom of the seventh.

The team is remembered for its depth, its pitching, and how it played under pressure. It was inducted into the Rocky River Athletics Hall of Fame in 2005.

**Team members:** Chris Blue, Matthew Brulport, Matthew Brunner, Michael Dickey, David Fortenbaugh, Jeremy Hitt, Anthony Martin, Christopher Meyer, Thomas Oxley, AJ Pease, Adam Petrovic, Eric Rocks, Russell Tigue, Jeff Werbeach

## The 1999 state runner-up team

A year later, the Pirates went back to the state finals determined to finish the job. Head coach James Rucki's 1999 team again reached the Ohio High School Athletic Association Division II state championship game, giving Rocky River back-to-back trips to the state finals and marking one of the strongest stretches in program history.

The postseason run included a 13-0 win in the regional semifinal. Led by its senior class, the team paired steady pitching with timely hitting, with contributions from Matt Brulport, Russ Tigue, Chris Meyer, Dave Fortenbaugh, Jeff Werbeach, Matt Novotny, and A.J. Pease.

The state final went to extra innings and was one of the closest championship games in school history. Rocky River lost on a walk-off and finished as Division II State Runner-Up for the second straight year, coming up just one run short.

Together, the 1998 and 1999 teams make the late 1990s the golden era of Rocky River baseball.

**Team members:** Chris Blue, Adam Petrovic, Cole Emde, Matt Brunner, Eric Rocks, Justin Bain, Nate Selak, Bill Weston, Matt Brulport, Russ Tigue, Dave Gutfranski, Chris Meyer, Dave Fortenbaugh, Jeff Werbeach, Matt Novotny, AJ Pease
